The idea

A hotel is only as good as the spot it was built on.

Ours isn't built on one. It is engineered like a yacht and registered as a vehicle, so it can be lifted off one farm and set down on another in a matter of hours. When the mountains go quiet, it is already somewhere else.

The signature

One residence, three seasons, one country

Montenegro is small enough that the mountains, the wine country and the sea are a few hours apart. So the same house stands in all three across a year, and it is never the one that sits empty.

June to September

The north

Durmitor, the Tara canyon, Biogradska Gora. Mountain pastures at 1,450m where the summer katun season runs.

April, May, October

The centre

Skadar Lake, Cetinje, the Crmnica wine valleys. The shoulder months, when the coast is cold and the mountains are wet.

Selected weeks

The south

A short, deliberately small stretch of coast. Kotor, Bar, Ulcinj, and only where a farm sits behind the water.

The residence travels on its own American chassis, by ordinary road, with nothing to dismantle and nothing to reconnect at the other end. It carries its own power, water and sanitation, which is what makes any of this possible.

On site

It brings its own everything

A pitch that can host one of these needs to be a piece of level ground and nothing else. That is what makes a field, a courtyard or a car park bookable at the same rate as a venue with services already in it.

  • Its own water, carried in a tank and filtered on board, so no standpipe is needed at the pitch.
  • Waste water stays with the unit in a grey tank and is emptied back at your own yard.
  • Its own refrigeration, holding temperature while the unit is towed and while it stands.
  • A battery bank runs the lights, the pumps and the till through a full outing.
  • Shore power is used where there is any, and is not needed where there is none.
  • Everything the unit serves with travels with it; the ground supplies nothing but the ground.

A field in the snow with no standpipe, no drain and no building on it. That is the pitch the specification above is written for, and it is why a unit like this can be booked where a catering tent cannot. The images on these pages are renders: the outside is drawn to a customer's brief, the chassis and the equipment behind it are the specification.

How it works

You buy it. You run it. That is the whole arrangement.

The unit is sold outright to your company. There is no management contract, no revenue share and no reporting back to us. You decide what it serves, where it goes, what it charges and who works in it, because it is yours.

What you get

A finished unit in your own livery, delivered to your gate with its documents. Ours is the build and the delivery; after that the diary is yours.

What we keep doing

Warranty on what we built, and parts. The fit-out is commercial equipment with European service, so anything inside it can be fixed by whoever services the rest of your bar.

One shell, two briefs. Nothing structural differs between these two rooms — the chassis, the width, the aisle and the standing height are the same unit. What changes is everything a customer of yours will actually notice.

This is the opposite of how the residences work, and the difference is deliberate. There we place the house on a farm and run the guests, because the owner is buying an income. Here you already have a business, a name and customers — you are buying equipment for it, not a service from us.

Included

The unit, and the first season with it

A trailer is a trailer. Anyone with a welder and a chassis can build one, and the ones that fail do not fail because the steel was wrong. They fail on the year that follows: the licence that took four months, the event whose calendar had closed in January, the supplier found in week nine. So the unit is handed over with that year written down.

Permits, for the countries you name

Which licence the unit needs where you intend to trade, who issues it, what it costs and how long it takes end to end. Written for the countries you give us rather than as a general page, because the answer in Croatia is not the answer in Austria.

What an outing is worth to you

The arithmetic further down this page, redone on your rates, your wages, your fuel and your season length — so when you quote a client you are quoting your own numbers, not ours.

Who to buy from

The suppliers behind the fit-out: kegs and cellar gas, glassware, coffee, consumables — and, which matters more, who services the machine in your region when it stops on a Saturday.

How the diary fills

Where the bookings actually come from: which organisers to approach, when their calendars close, what a deposit and a weather clause should look like, and what to charge for the outing that runs long.

It comes with the unit at no separate charge, and it is a document rather than a service: we are not running your diary, taking a share or standing between you and your customer. It is not legal advice either — your own lawyer signs the licence off. It is the list of things you would otherwise spend a first season finding out one at a time.

Delivery

To your yard, not to a port

The unit is built in Istanbul and delivered to the address you give us — Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain, anywhere in the EU. Customs clearance and the import paperwork are handled as part of the delivery, and the price we quote is the price at your gate.

It arrives ready to work: on its own wheels, within towing dimensions, tested and cleaned down before it leaves the yard.

Buying it

Leasing and fleet terms

Leasing for an EU company

A unit is movable equipment with a serial number and an invoice, which is what a leasing company writes against. We prepare the invoice and the full build specification, and work with the provider you already bank with — tell us what else your lessor wants to see and we put it together.

Fleets and white label

Caterers and event agencies ordering more than one unit get fleet terms and a build slot reserved across the order. Units can be delivered unbranded, or finished in your own livery from the factory rather than wrapped afterwards.

Terms are agreed per order, in writing. Tell us the number of units and the country the company is registered in, and you get a quotation rather than a range.

Before you ask the price

Questions we are asked first

Whose name is on the outside?

Yours. The unit is delivered unbranded, or finished in your own livery at the factory — painted into the build rather than wrapped over it afterwards. Nothing of ours appears on it.

Can I tow it with the vehicle I already have?

Both units are built within European towing dimensions, and the loaded weight is confirmed in writing before the build starts. You know which vehicle and which licence it needs before you commit to anything.

Can the layout be changed?

Inside the shell, yes: which side the counter faces, how many taps, which machine, where the till sits. The chassis, the shell and the services are fixed, and that is what holds the price where it is.

How long does it take to build?

A build slot is reserved against your order and the date comes with the quotation. We would rather give you a date we can hold than a lead time that reads well in an email.

What is not in the price?

The vehicle that tows it, your insurance, and whatever your own municipality asks of a business serving food or drink. The unit, its fit-out and delivery to your gate are in it.

What happens when something breaks?

The fit-out is commercial equipment you can already get parts for in Europe, not fittings of our own design. A machine, a tap or a fridge is replaced by whoever services the rest of your bar.

    Price and economics

    What the price is made of, and when it comes back

    Every component we costed, what each one adds, and the arithmetic of a season — separately for the bar and for the coffee bar. Disagree with a line rather than with the conclusion.

    Why the price moves

    The shell is ours. The equipment is your choice.

    Two units off the same drawing can differ by twenty thousand euro, and all of that difference sits in the equipment. Some buyers already own machines. Some have a supplier they have worked with for years. Some want one particular espresso machine and nothing else. We build the trailer around whatever you decide, and the price follows that decision rather than a price list of ours.

    If you bring your own equipment, its line simply comes out of the quotation. What stays is the shell, the services and the fitting — and we will say plainly whether what you have will work in a trailer that moves.

    What we build

    The shell, and everything that makes it road-legal

    This part does not change with the fit-out. It is the same trailer under a draft bar and under an espresso counter: the chassis, the body, the services, the paint and the papers.

    The draft bar

    What goes into pouring

    Built around the glycol line, because that is what decides whether the last pour at midnight is the same temperature as the first one at noon.

    The espresso bar

    Where the choice of machine changes everything

    The espresso machine and the grinders are the single largest variable in the whole quotation. A mid-range two-group and a La Marzocco Linea are eight thousand euro apart, and the trailer around them is identical.

    The price

    What a unit comes to

    Delivered to your gate, in your own livery, with the papers. The spread inside each line is the equipment, not our margin — that stays the same across the whole range.

    The bar, in numbers

    What a draft bar earns

    The client brings the kegs, so there is no drinks cost on our side. In exchange the outing costs more to run: a pub has to be towed, set up and taken down by two people.

    The coffee bar, in numbers

    What an espresso bar earns

    One barista, three hours on site, a hundred and fifty drinks — the package the Alberta operator sells. Here the drinks do cost something, because you are the one supplying them.

The rate of €1,450 an outing is an estimate for western Europe; the only rates we can point at are the two North American operators who publish theirs. Both will move when we have invoices and European operators to compare against, and we will publish what changes.

    TinyArc Group

    About Tiny Mansion

    It started with a trailer built by hand, over two years, mostly wrongly.

    What came out of it was a simple observation: the trouble with a beautiful place to stay is that it can only ever be in one place.

    So we built the other thing. A residence engineered like a yacht, registered as a vehicle, able to run a full week with no power, water or drainage connected to it. It stands on a farm for a season and then it goes somewhere else.

    We don't own land and don't intend to. Across Europe there are farms with owners, with land, and with almost no income, and they are better places to wake up than anything we could buy. We work with them instead.

    The first twenty-five residences deploy across Montenegro from Q3 2026: the mountains of the north, the wine country in the middle, and a small number of coastal sites in the south. Croatia follows, then further afield.
