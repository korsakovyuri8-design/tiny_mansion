Mobile bars and coffee units

Built to the same discipline as the residences, on a shorter chassis of its own and fitted out to serve. Within European towing dimensions, delivered to your pitch anywhere in the EU.

Why not shorter

A two-group machine, two grinders, a sink, under-counter refrigeration, water tanks and the battery bank all have to live in the same box. At 16 ft they compete for the same space and a second barista has nowhere to stand. The extra half metre gives the tanks and batteries their own compartment and keeps the working line clear.

Why not longer

21 ft is our house platform, sized for living space. For a serving unit the extra length adds weight and cost without adding anything you serve from.

Width and height are within road-legal limits, so the unit moves on public roads without an escort or a special movement permit. Layouts, both service-only and service with guest seating, are drawn to this footprint and adapted to your equipment.

At the pitch

It brings its own water. It expects power.

A pitch needs to be level ground with a socket or a generator on it, and nothing else. Water and waste travel with the unit, which is what makes a field, a courtyard or a car park bookable at the same rate as a venue with services already in it.

  • A 150 litre fresh tank, pump, filtration and softening sized for espresso equipment. It travels empty and fills on site, so no standpipe is needed at the pitch.
  • A 180 litre grey tank — deliberately larger than the fresh one, so it cannot overflow before the fresh runs out. Emptied at any standard point.
  • Its own refrigeration, holding temperature while the unit is towed and while it stands.
  • Shore power, 230 V 16 A single phase, with a distribution board, RCD protection and an external socket.
  • Generator input as standard, so the unit works at a festival site with no mains at all.
  • Everything the unit serves with travels with it; the ground supplies level and electricity, and nothing else.

Power is the one thing the standard unit does not carry. If you need it to work where there is neither mains nor a generator, that is the off-grid build — solar, a lithium bank, an inverter — sized to your equipment and priced separately. The specification page sets out what that costs you in weight.

A field in the snow with no standpipe, no drain and no building on it — a generator behind the camera, or a socket on the fence. That is the pitch the specification above is written for, and it is why a unit like this can be booked where a catering tent cannot. The images on these pages are renders: the outside is drawn to a customer's brief, the chassis and the equipment behind it are the specification.

Inside the shell, yes: which side the counter faces, how many taps, which machine, where the till sits. The chassis, the shell and the services are fixed, and that is what holds the price where it is.

How long does it take to build?

What is not in the price?

The vehicle that tows it, your insurance, and whatever your own municipality asks of a business serving food or drink. The unit, its fit-out and delivery to your gate are in it.

What happens when something breaks?

The fit-out is commercial equipment you can already get parts for in Europe, not fittings of our own design. A machine, a tap or a fridge is replaced by whoever services the rest of your bar.

    Four working stations in 11 m²

    Barbershop

    Three chairs and a backwash

    Four chairs do not fit on this platform. A barber needs clear floor around the chair, and a backwash unit is not optional.

    Power

    Off the grid as standard, and no generator

    Both configurations run off-grid as standard and both accept shore power where it exists. This is where a salon differs from a bar: an espresso machine and a keg fridge draw continuously, so those units expect a socket. A salon's load is light and comes in pulses, so it can carry its own.

    Nail studio

    LiFePO4 battery, 15 kWh. Inverter 3 kW. Solar 1.5 to 2 kW on the roof. Typical draw 0.6 to 0.9 kW; a shift takes 6 to 9 kWh, or 10 to 15 with air conditioning in summer.

    Curing lamps run in pulses and a dust extractor draws 60 to 100 W. The unit charges overnight at base and works the day on the battery, with solar covering most of the cooling in summer.

    Barbershop

    The same 15 kWh battery and 1.5 to 2 kW of solar, but a 5 kW inverter with 10 kW of peak. Dryers are brushless, 1.0 to 1.2 kW each. Water and cabin heat come from a diesel heater rather than an element.

    Hair dryers are the hard part, and they are solved in layers rather than with a generator.

    • Brushless dryers do the same work at half the draw, because the air speed does the drying rather than the heat: three of them peak at 3.0 to 3.6 kW instead of 6 to 7.
    • A load manager stops all three running at full power at once, which brings the peak down again.
    • Dryers run for minutes, not hours. Over a full shift, drying accounts for 2 to 3 kWh — what matters is the peak, and that is what the 10 kW of inverter headroom is for.
    • The larger consumer is not the dryers but hot water. An electric instant heater would draw 5 to 7 kW for as long as the basin runs, so the water is heated with diesel instead: the same fuel as the tow vehicle, almost no electricity, and cabin heat in winter as well.
    Water

    Small tanks, because the water goes round

    A closed-loop recirculation system filters and returns the water, so the tanks are there for make-up and the first fill rather than for a full day's supply.

    Against the conventional 400 and 500 litre arrangement this saves roughly 700 kg — and that saving is precisely what makes the off-grid pack fit under 3,500 kg.

    Climate and air

    More air handling than a coffee unit, not less

    • Inverter split heat pump, heating and cooling from one unit.
    • Insulation: 60 mm PIR in the walls, 80 mm in the roof, 50 mm in the floor, a thermal break at the frame, double glazing.
    • Nail studio: source extraction at every station plus ridge ducting. Acetone vapour and filing dust are the reason this configuration carries more air handling than a unit that serves coffee.
    • Barbershop: ridge extraction, with the diesel heater providing cabin heat.

    Permits

    A unit on wheels is still not a building

    The principle is the same as for the bars: movable property rather than construction, so there is no building permit. What you need instead is the right to be on the land, a registered business, a local placement approval where the municipality requires one, and registered chassis and insurance.

    One difference matters for a salon. A beauty or barbering service carries its own sanitary and hygiene licensing, separate from the food rules that apply to bars and coffee units, and it is usually the sterilisation and waste requirements an inspector looks at first. The unit is built to meet them: a sterilisation cabinet, a hand basin separate from the service basins, and closed waste storage. The licence itself is issued to you as the operator, and your municipality or health authority confirms what it requires.

    Where it goes

    The technician is the product

    At 85% occupancy the technicians take 45% of revenue — about €4,850 of €10,770. Materials are 10 to 12%, and fuel and local fees are most of the rest.

    Technician pay is the largest line by a wide margin, and it is a share of revenue rather than of profit. That is how this trade works: the person is what the client is booking.

    Before you use these

    What the table leaves out

    • Payroll taxes are not in it. If technicians are employed, contributions sit on top; if they are registered as sole traders working for a percentage, which is the norm in this trade, the picture is different. Your accountant decides which applies to you.

    • The first two or three months will not reach 70%. A new format takes time to fill a diary, and six months to working occupancy is the realistic assumption.
    The case that changes it

    If you are the technician

    Most buyers of a unit like this work in it. Then the 45% is not paid out, and the arithmetic looks quite different — but exactly how different depends on how many of the four stations are worked by someone on a percentage.

    Legal status

    A unit is not a building

    It is a registered trailer on a twin-axle chassis with plates and papers, and for as long as it stands on its wheels it remains movable property.

    The principle

    What that one fact changes

    You are not applying for a building permit, you are not going through a design and construction approval, and the unit does not become part of the land it stands on. You keep the right to move it, sell it or take it with you.

    The line to respect is the same everywhere: a unit on wheels, without foundations and without hard connections to mains services, is a vehicle. A unit set on blocks, with the wheels removed and pipes buried into the ground, starts to look like a building to an inspector, whatever the paperwork says.

    Instead

    What you do need

    • The right to be on the land: your own property, or a written agreement with the owner. On public ground, a lease or a placement approval from the municipality.
    • A registered business and a hospitality or trade licence, with the food-safety requirements that apply to any mobile serving point.
    • A local placement approval, where the municipality requires one for a unit standing on the same spot through a season.
    • Registered chassis and insurance for towing on public roads.
    Montenegro

    Temporary objects, decided by the municipality

    Montenegrin municipalities regulate this through their own decisions on temporary objects, adopted under the national planning and construction law. Each municipality has a Programme of Temporary Objects setting out where they may stand and under what urban conditions, and it covers private land as well as municipal land.

    • Placement is approved by the municipality, not through a construction procedure.
    • On private land the approval typically runs up to one year and not less than two months, renewable, and is tied to the land keeping its current planned use.
    • A local communal fee is payable for placing a temporary object, on private land as well as municipal land.
    • An approval to carry out the activity is required alongside the placement approval.
    • The coastal strip is Morsko dobro and sits under a separate regime with its own authority. A unit standing on the coast is a different procedure from one standing inland.
    Croatia

    The wheels are the whole argument

    Croatian practice draws the distinction sharply. A mobile unit on wheels is a movable object, not permanently attached to the ground, so it does not fall under the Construction Act and no building permit is issued for it. A kiosk or a prefabricated structure built on private land does need one, which is exactly why the wheels matter.

    • The unit may be placed where the spatial plan for that location allows it, and the local planning department is the office to ask.
    • What turns a mobile unit into a building in the eyes of an inspector is permanence: foundations, buried service connections, and continuous use as fixed premises.
    • Agricultural land is the strictest category. It is intended for agricultural production and the buildings that serve it, so anything permanent there is a separate and much harder conversation. A unit on wheels avoids that conversation as long as it stays mobile.
    • Serving guests requires the hospitality activity to be registered, with the food-safety requirements that go with it.
    Georgia

    A category built for exactly this

    Georgian law has a non-stationary trading place: a temporary structure made of assembled elements or a mobile system, as opposed to a permanent building. Building permits in Georgia are tied to building classes, and a mobile serving unit is not treated as a construction project in the way a permanent café building is.

    • The unit is registered and operated as a vehicle plus a registered business, not as construction.
    • Placement is agreed with the landowner on private land and with the municipality on public land.
    • Local rules on trading objects and their placement are set at municipal level, so Tbilisi and Batumi are asked separately.
    • Food-safety registration with the National Food Agency applies to serving, as it does to any mobile point.

    This page describes the framework we build to and the questions we help you answer. It is not legal advice. The requirements on your specific plot are confirmed by your own lawyer or your municipality before the unit is placed, and we give you the written guide to take to them.

    About Tiny Mansion

    It started with a trailer built by hand, over two years, mostly wrongly.

    What came out of it was a simple observation: the trouble with a beautiful place to stay is that it can only ever be in one place.

    So we built the other thing. A residence engineered like a yacht, registered as a vehicle, able to run a full week with no power, water or drainage connected to it. It stands on a farm for a season and then it goes somewhere else.

    We don't own land and don't intend to. Across Europe there are farms with owners, with land, and with almost no income, and they are better places to wake up than anything we could buy. We work with them instead.

    The first twenty-five residences deploy across Montenegro from Q1 2027: the mountains of the north, the wine country in the middle, and a small number of coastal sites in the south. Croatia follows, then further afield.
